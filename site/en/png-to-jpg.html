PNG to JPG — when it shrinks the file, and when it should stay PNG

For photos, JPG is the right call. For text and screenshots, PNG usually is.

한국어 English 日本語

"PNG files are not accepted." "File too large." Those are the two usual reasons to turn a PNG into a JPG. But converting is not always an improvement — it depends on what is in the picture.

PNG and JPG are good at different things

  • JPG stores smooth, photographic images compactly by throwing away detail your eye barely notices.
  • PNG throws nothing away. For images made of text, lines and flat colour — screenshots, charts, logos — PNG is sharper and often smaller. It can also keep a transparent background.

So a photo saved as PNG — common with screenshots of photos and some capture tools — shrinks dramatically as a JPG. A screenshot full of text, on the other hand, gets fuzzy edges around the letters and barely shrinks.

Converting several files

  • Drop the PNG files into Bulk Image Resizer — as many as you like.
  • Under Format, choose JPEG.
  • Start with size on Keep original and quality at the default 0.80.
  • Press Convert. Each file shows how much it shrank. Save them one by one or use Download as zip.

If a result came out larger than the original, the result line says why — usually it is the text-and-lines case above. Keep those as PNG.

Transparent backgrounds turn white

Convert a logo or icon with a transparent background to JPG and the transparent area is filled with white, because JPG has no transparency. That is fine on a white page; on a coloured background, keep it as PNG or choose WebP, which keeps transparency.

What about WebP instead?

If the place you are uploading to accepts WebP, it is the better choice: smaller than JPG at the same quality, and it keeps transparency. Some forms still only take JPG or PNG, so check first.
